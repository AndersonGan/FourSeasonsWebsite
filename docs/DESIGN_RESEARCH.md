# Design research & creative direction

## What the restaurant already "looks like"

Public descriptions of the storefront (brief §7, reference [8]): **red signage and awning,
green bamboo, Chinese characters, bold outlined lettering.** That is a strong, ownable
starting point — the site's master identity is therefore **restaurant red + bamboo green on
rice paper**, never swapped out by seasonal theming.

## Local competitors (see BUSINESS_RESEARCH §7)

Patterns observed across local Chinese restaurant listings: menus as photographed PDFs or
third-party listings, no searchable HTML menu, phone number buried, no visual identity beyond
stock photography. **Opportunities:** crawlable menu with item-number search; phone and
directions in one tap; an identity that looks like *this* restaurant, not a template.

## Reference studies (principles only — no assets or identities copied)

| Reference | What we took (principle) | What we avoided |
|---|---|---|
| BAO London | Confident, characterful illustration carrying the brand instead of photos | Their mascot/linework style |
| Din Tai Fung | Dumplings as hero objects; calm, generous menu typography | Luxury tone |
| RedFarm | Playfulness inside a serious menu | Neon / NYC graffiti |
| Ugly Dumpling (Stefan) | Bold flat colour fields, food drawn as shapes | Their palette and characters |
| Zoé Tong (Overstory) | Editorial serif + modern sans pairing; asymmetric layouts | Fine-dining restraint |
| Hao's Bistro (cgica) | Packaging-led identity, seals as brand marks | Their seal artwork |
| Traditional Chinese print & takeout packaging | Double-rule frames, woodblock botanicals, vermilion seals, lattice borders, dotted menu leaders | Stereotype "chop-suey" fonts, random clip art |

## Three internal directions explored

1. **Contemporary illustrated takeout** — red fields, the folded takeout box, chopsticks,
   steam. *Strength:* instantly says "takeout," joyful. *Risk:* fast-food feel if used alone.
2. **Four-season botanical / woodblock** — plum, lotus, harvest moon, pines; seals; rice-paper
   texture. *Strength:* gives the name meaning and makes the site feel alive through the year.
   *Risk:* too precious for a neighborhood kitchen.
3. **Modern editorial neighborhood restaurant** — big Fraunces headlines, numbered dish lists,
   asymmetric grids, printed-menu leaders. *Strength:* readability and hierarchy, a "real
   place" feel. *Risk:* cold without illustration.

**Chosen: a combination.** Direction 3 provides structure and typography (menu usability is
15 rubric points), direction 1 provides the hero and colour blocks (Mar Far, family dinners,
takeout), and direction 2 is the seasonal accent layer and footer/page art. Reasoning: the
editorial system keeps real customer tasks immediate, while the illustration layers give the
originality and warmth the brief asks for — and the seasonal layer can be switched off
without breaking anything.

## Decisions worth recording

- **Photos + illustration.** Four real dish photos from the restaurant's DoorDash listing (owner
  request) are cut out from their white backgrounds so plates sit directly on the rice-paper page.
  Illustration still carries the identity and covers dishes without photos (e.g. Mar Far).
- **Find Your Flavor** (brief §14) was *not* built: it would require tagging dishes "crispy",
  "sweet/tangy" etc. — editorial claims the owner hasn't approved. The menu instead offers a
  verified-only "Spice adjustable" filter.
- **No online checkout.** The takeout list is a memo for the phone call, stated three times in
  the UI.
- **Map is click-to-load** to protect performance and privacy.
